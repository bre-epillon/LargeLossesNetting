Gross -to -net claims model

Netting down through both excess of loss covers and both quota shares, the technical account that follows from it, and a side by side comparison of loss scenarios. Every parameter is editable and results recalculate immediately.

Every loss in the programme

Amounts are in millions, carried to nine digits. Each treaty has its own add a loss here button, so a claim goes straight onto the year it attaches to; the Treaty column moves one afterwards if it was filed against the wrong year. The order inside a treaty is the order the tool applies it in — use the arrows to change it, because aggregate deductibles erode in that order. Ext QS is that claim’s own external quota share order; leave it blank to cede at the treaty order.

Keeping the register

Save this tool with the register writes a fresh copy of this HTML file with the list above baked into it, so the next person to open that copy starts with it already there. It saves the register only — treaty terms and assumptions stay as filed. Use Save scenario in the header if you want to keep everything else too.

3 Losses already hit

Claims that already went through this treaty before the ones below, each split between what has been paid and what is still outstanding. The programme is run over them on a clean opening and wherever it leaves the deductibles, the reinstatements and the aggregate limit becomes the opening position for the losses below — so the second loss into the treaty is netted knowing the first one already ate the retention. Leave the list empty to type the opening figures in by hand instead.

What these losses leave behind

4 Losses

Enter each event and the tool nets it down straight away. FAC is the facultative recovery on that claim; it comes off in full before the treaty, so both excess of loss covers, both quota shares and the retention all work on the claim after FAC — a 27m claim with 7m of facultative puts 20m into the treaty, and on the paid side the facultative is collected first rather than spread over the payments. Outstanding is the part of the claim still reserved rather than paid — leave it nil for a fully paid claim. Sequence matters: aggregate deductibles, free reinstatements and the aggregate limit erode in the order shown.

Main coverage — layer by layer

Threshold is the claim in excess of the layer's attachment point. Layer recovery is capped at the limit and reduced by the aggregate deductible still available. Reinstatement premium is charged at the rate on line up to the free capacity (reinstatements × limit + AAD) and at the loaded rate above it.

Additional coverage — section by section

A section that is not triggered by the loss type, or that fails its franchise test, recovers nil and consumes no reinstatement. The shaded row under each loss shows the per event and aggregate limit test.

Premium is charged pro rata to actual GNWP against the reference GNWP, subject to the minimum and deposit. The minimum only bites where actual GNWP falls below the reference by more than the minimum and deposit percentage.

Excess of loss premium and minimum & deposit test

Premium at 100% is the limit at the rate on line, taken at the order placed. Premium at M&D is the same figure at each cover's minimum and deposit percentage. Which of the two is charged depends on actual GNWP against the reference, per the test under each cover. Sections whose rate on line is quoted as a multiple of section A carry no premium of their own, matching the workbook.
